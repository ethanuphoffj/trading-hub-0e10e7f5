01 · SCOPE — WHY THIS GUIDE EXISTS

02 · THE SILVER BULLET FRAMEWORK — SIX COMPONENTS

What the setup actually is

Silver Bullet is the most famous setup built from the ICT price-reading framework. Six sequential components, then a timing rule. Learn it to read it — the verdict on each piece comes in §4.

THE SILVER BULLET SETUP — run the stops, then fill the gap. 1 · liquidity (obvious low, stops resting under it) → 2 · sweep (dips below, takes them) → 3 · displacement (fast counter-move) → 4 · fair value gap (the zone price skipped) → 5 · entry (retrace into the gap) → 6 · target (opposing liquidity). Bottom band: the three "special windows" as taught — London 3–4am · NY AM 10–11am · NY PM 2–3pm — tested: no edge (§4).

💬The claims attached to it: a daily-repeatable income model, win rates of 70–80%. Both were tested directly. Neither survived — the real numbers are in §4.
03 · THE TEST — HOW THE VERDICT WAS EARNED

25,000 configurations, five layers of rigor

Because ICT teaches concepts instead of numbers, every trader fills in their own parameters — what counts as a sweep, how big a gap must be, where the stop goes, which bias to use. Multiplied out, that's ≈258 million distinct versions. The study ran a uniform random sample of 25,000 of them (polling logic — you don't call every voter), plus the three named versions traders actually use: the 5m textbook, the 1m textbook, and the loose "as-traded" version.

Rigor layerWhat it did
Real costsEvery trade paid commission; every stop-out paid slippage on top.
Honesty ruleIf a candle touched stop AND target in the same minute → counted as a loss.
Coin-flip benchmarkEvery config compared against literal coin flips with identical risk settings.
Luck correctionStatistical adjustment across all 25,000 — run enough backtests and something looks amazing by accident.
Cross-market checkSurvivors run cold on ES with zero retuning.
Holdout lockThe last 2 years of data never touched by any tuning — run exactly once, at the end.
5m textbook version: trades, makes a little money — dies at the risk-adjusted bar.
1m textbook version: reaches the final stage — dies on the ES cross-check.
As-traded (loose) version: any recent swing counts as liquidity, re-enters after exits, ≈1,996 trades/yr — survives every stage into the 1.7%.
Holdout results: best survivor $455K in 2 years on one contract (Sharpe 1.3, 24/24 positive months); as-traded $251K. Caveats: strong bull years + paper fills — treat as a ceiling, not an expectation.

05 · THE SIX SURVIVING RULES

The six surviving rules — the profitable configuration

Across the funnel, the holdout, and the cross-market check, the profile that survives is the simplest one: one real signal, simple entry, basic risk management, traded a lot.

1 · Require the liquidity sweep. The only rule that adds edge. No sweep, no setup.
2 · Take direction from the sweep. Fade the side that just got run — that's where the information is.
3 · Enter on the 1-minute gap the reversal leaves behind. 5m versions underperformed at every stage.
4 · Trade it often. The edge per trade is small — the money comes from frequency.
5 · Drop the 15m bias. It makes everything worse (69 → 0.16).
6 · Don't wait for the special hours. The edge is not in the clock.
⚠️House caveat on rule 4: "trade it often" is the surviving profile for a standalone mean-reversion machine grinding ~2,000 trades a year. T1 is not that machine — T1 trades A+ setups through a 13-gate checklist. The lesson T1 takes is narrower: when the sweep + 1m FVG appears inside an already-validated T1 zone, that's the highest-probability trigger form there is. Frequency without the T1 context is somebody else's system.
